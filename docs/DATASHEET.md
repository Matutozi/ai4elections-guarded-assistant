# Datasheet: data used by the Guarded Election Assistant

The prototype creates **no datasets**. It uses the hackathon's **Synthetic Starter Data Pack** as it is, read-only, and writes only run-time records: the gate's audit log and the collation ledger, both in `.run/` and not part of the repository. This sheet describes the pack as this project uses it. The organisers' data dictionary is at [data/synthetic/DATA_DICTIONARY.md](../data/synthetic/DATA_DICTIONARY.md).

1. **Motivation.** The organisers (Rise Networks) created the pack for hackathon prototyping without touching real electoral data. We use it to give the assistant's tools something realistic to act on, and to measure the result checks.

2. **Composition.** It's entirely synthetic: party labels A–E, polling-unit IDs `SIM-#####`, invented LGAs and wards; states and zones are real names, used for realism.

   | File | Records | Used for |
   |---|---|---|
   | `voter_info_faq_multilingual.csv` | 20 questions in English, plus Pidgin, Hausa, Yoruba and Igbo drafts; answers are blank placeholders | `faq.answer` |
   | `polling_units_synthetic.csv` | 900 units | `units.lookup`; the register figure for each result check |
   | `results_synthetic.csv` | 900 sheets; about 5% with injected inconsistencies | `results.unit`, `results.flags`; sheets sent in the evaluation |
   | `anomaly_labels_HELD_OUT.csv` | 900 labels: 858 `none`, 16 accredited-exceeds-registered, 12 round numbers, 8 sum mismatch, 5 duplicated, 1 digit transposition | Measuring the result checks only |
   | `misinformation_claims_synthetic.jsonl` | 300 claims labelled true, false, misleading or unverifiable | `claims.check` |
   | `incident_reports_synthetic.csv` | 400 reports | `incidents.summary`, `incident.escalate` |
   | `accessibility_barriers_survey_synthetic.csv` | 500 simulated respondents: state, settlement, age band, gender, disability type, language, barrier, confidence | `survey.aggregate` (and `survey.respondents`, which policy denies) |
   | `logistics_deliveries_synthetic.csv` | 300 deliveries | Not used |

   **Personal or sensitive data:** none real. The survey simulates sensitive attributes (disability, gender, age). We treat them as if they were real: they're released only as aggregates over five or more respondents, and never by row.

3. **Collection process.** Generated by the organisers' script `tools/generate_synthetic_data.py`. We did no collection.

4. **Preprocessing.** None to the files. At run time, counts are parsed as integers. Survey aggregates suppress any group with fewer than five respondents, whatever the request asks for.

5. **Uses.**
   - **Intended:** exercising the tools, the policy and the evaluation.
   - **Must not be used for:** any inference about real elections, places, parties or people, as the organisers state.
   - The held-out labels are used only to measure the result checks. They are fixed rules, and nothing was fitted to the labels.

6. **Distribution.** As distributed in the hackathon repository, under CC BY 4.0, unchanged here.

7. **Maintenance.** The organisers maintain the pack. Run-time records in `.run/` are local and can be deleted at any time.

8. **Known limitations and biases.**
   - The FAQ's translations are drafts and its answers are empty.
   - The injected result anomalies are simple and few: 42 of 900, five types.
   - Distributions are simplistic, as the organisers note.
   - The 500-respondent survey spread over many states makes many cross-tab groups small, so most fine-grained aggregates are suppressed.
   - The claims are in English (195) and Pidgin (105) only. A message in Hausa, Yoruba or Igbo has no fact-check in its own language to match.
